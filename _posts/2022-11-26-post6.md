The year 2022 is coming to an end. The Hematoscope Lab is thrilled on the progress made during our time in operation 2021-2022. Here's a sneak peak on what we have been doing and where we are now.  
<br>

Our team includes now 10 members. `Johanna Heikkinen` and `Anni Dohlen` are the hard-working microscope technicians responsible for creating the image data our lab is established on. Their indispensable work ensures that patients slides are digitized 24/7 with high-resolution.

### Achievements so far
Without doubt, the most important achievement is creating an environment, where researchers can develop their careers, learn new skills, and enjoy working together. As a young researcher, I feel overwhelmed by the trust of our collaborators to contribute their time and effort to our common cause.
<br><br>
**Main achievements**
1. The world's largest digital dataset of digital May-Grünwald-Giemsa (MGG) slides. These are analogical to H&E-stained slides commonly used in histopathology. MGG-stained slides are challenging to digitize as these require immersion oil and ≥63-100x microscope objectives. By the end of the year, we have digized over 40 000 bone marrow slides including all pediatric and almost all adult hematological patients of the Helsinki University Hospital district for 2009-2022. Next in line - cerebrospinal and peripheral blood samples.
2. Image analysis algorithms. We deeply believe in supervised learning. Our team has created a pipeline to optimize image annotation and annotated thousands of images. We have dedicated this Fall to improve computational speed. The speed up for some algorithms has been around 10x, which likely does not say anything but is fundamental if an algorithm needs to be run for tens of thousands of samples. Most of our models are currently being validated and we look forward to present these in more detail in 2023.
3. The Hematoscope app. My #1 reason of frustration in cancer research is the lack of translation. In other words, how can we translate the innovative work of researchers to clinical practice? This is something we have discussed thoroughly this year and the Hematoscope app is our solution. Simply put, it is THE software connecting the bioanalyst, the hematopathologist, the electronic health records, and the deep learning networks together. We will launch this officially in 2023.
4. Publications. We will finalize 2 manuscripts during 2023, which will represent the flagship papers of the Hematoscope Lab. Stay tuned!
