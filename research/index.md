# <i class="fas fa-microscope"></i>Research

In hematology, there is an acute unmet need for automated machine vision to speed up routine diagnostics due to persistant shortage of trained laboratory physicians and increasing number of analyzed samples per year. Our recent results suggest that bone marrow samples have an unexplored potential in disease subclassification, with direct possibilities of translation to patient prognostication (Brück et al, 2021).  
<br>
We have set up a digital slide scanner in the Helsinki University Hospital laboratory (HUSLAB), and our first aim is to digitize all 60 000 archived MGG-stained bone marrow and cerebrospinal fluid slides. In 2021, we have already digitized 15 000 samples making it by far the world’s largest hematocytological digital image archive. The second aim of the project is to develop image analysis algorithms to decode cell morphologies present in the hematocytological samples. Our software will detect and automatically classify 15 different cell types as well as any signs of dysplasia, and further classify samples into urgent and non-urgent groups to help prioritizing clinical examination. In addition, the software provides possibilities of identifying novel biomarkers for treatment.  
<br>
<img src="images/slidescanner.jpg" alt="Slide scanner" style="align: center" />
<br>
Computer-assisted diagnostics will reduce time required for slide examination by over 50 %, provide deeper information on risk and treatment stratification, and help us to better understand the biology of various disease phenotypes. The extensive image archive will serve both in terms of quality control of bone marrow products, standardize the training of specializing physicians, and improve future collaboration with hospitals, research groups, and companies. In summary, we will build a globally unique image dataset of hematological patients with the ultimate aim to develop and integrate severely needed algorithms into clinical practice.
